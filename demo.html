How Typographic Micro-Adjustments Unlock Effortless Reading for Neurodivergent Minds

By Dr. Clara Vance, Department of Cognitive Neurobiology • Published October 19, 2026 • 8 min read

For over two centuries, typography has been optimized for high-volume mechanical printing presses rather than the biological architecture of human visual perception. When standard webpages force tight letter kerning, rigid vertical rhythms, and uniform glyph geometry onto the screen, individuals with dyslexia, ADHD, and visual processing sensitivities expend up to 40% more cognitive energy simply resolving character boundaries.

This cognitive overhead is known as the "visual crowding effect." In neurotypical readers, the brain effortlessly suppresses adjacent typographic noise during rapid saccadic movements. However, for readers experiencing typographic distortion, neighboring letters bleed into one another, creating optical illusions commonly described as text floating, vibrating, or swapping order.
